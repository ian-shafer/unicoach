# Side by side: one cost question, four answers (2026-11-04)

**Method.** The unicoach answer was produced by Ian in the live app for a
Washington student whose list holds UW Seattle, Western Washington, Cal Poly
SLO, UC Berkeley, San Diego State and Oregon State, with a household income on
file of $110,000 or more. The three competitor answers were produced the same
evening by the coordinator in the Claude desktop app's built-in browser, logged
out of every service, with one typed line:

> I'm a high school senior in Washington state. My family makes about $120k a
> year. How much would it cost me per year to go to UW Seattle, Western
> Washington, Cal Poly SLO, UC Berkeley, San Diego State, and Oregon State?

Google's AI Overview was reached through a two-school search ("how much does UC
Berkeley cost for a Washington resident vs UW Seattle") because a direct
six-school URL tripped Google's bot check. ChatGPT answered without an account.
Gemini answered without an account in its Flash-Lite mode. Nothing was retried
or regenerated; each is the first answer given.

This is one trial, not a study. It is enough to show the shape of the gap, not
its size.

## What each got right and wrong (coordinator's reading)

**unicoach.** Right: whose numbers (aided first-years), what a net price is,
loans never subtracted, the in-state basis of the federal net price, the $110k+
income-band net price at the two in-state schools, the at-home arrangement and
whose assumption the $0 is. Wrong or missing: every figure two to three years
behind the schools' own pages (UW ~$2k low, Berkeley ~$14k low); "no comparable
out-of-state figure" at Oregon State when WUE exists; no next step at any
out-of-state school; no four-year view; the follow-up asks living plan rather
than the aid inputs; two provenance paragraphs before the first number.

**Google AI Overview.** Right: current 2026-27 line items from the schools' own
pages, the nonresident supplemental tuition named, the "establishing residency
is unlikely" warning. Wrong or missing: only answers the two schools in the
search; "saving roughly $53,000 per year" treats sticker as the price paid;
offers no net price for any income.

**ChatGPT.** Right: 2026-27 sticker for all six, Cal Poly's major-based range,
the WUE flag at Oregon State phrased conditionally ("if you qualify for a WUE
scholarship"), a four-year table, and a follow-up that asks household size,
savings and living plan. Wrong or missing: no net price anywhere; "has
substantial financial aid, but nonresident aid eligibility is more restricted"
is hedged to the point of saying nothing; the Berkeley figure (~$93,944) could
not be verified against Berkeley's page.

**Gemini Flash-Lite.** Right: the in-state advantage, WUE named at Oregon State.
Wrong: Oregon State at $38,000–45,000 presents a competitive scholarship as the
default price; "UW Husky Promise … for middle-income Washington residents" is
false for a $120k family (Husky Promise follows Pell and the Washington College
Grant); Berkeley $75,000–85,000 is below the school's own 2026-27 total; ranges
throughout with no source.

(money never paid back); loans are never subtracted.

- UW–Seattle and Western Washington charge you in-state tuition and fees — their
  lower tier.
- Cal Poly SLO, UC Berkeley, San Diego State, and Oregon State charge you
  out-of-state — their higher tier. Because of that, the after-aid net price the
  federal government publishes is on an in-state basis and doesn't apply to you

  instead.
